# Research section: fresh design and content quality bar

**Scope:** Desktop review of `/research/`, the published programme map and representative reading pages. Mobile review is out of scope for this pass.

This bar is specific to this review and was derived from the current site. It sets the acceptance conditions for visual, navigation, diagram and research-content changes.

## Reader outcome

A technically experienced reader can decide where to start, stay oriented in a long programme, read without the interface squeezing the prose, and distinguish evidence from inference without leaving the page to decode the design.

## Pass conditions

### 1. Entry and hierarchy

- The research index and programme map explain the subject, intended reader and next useful action in the first desktop viewport at 1200 × 900.
- Each page has one clear primary action. Secondary actions remain visually subordinate.
- Programme titles wrap deliberately; no isolated word or awkward fragment dominates the first screen.
- Status, chapter counts and reading time help a reader choose. They do not compete with the title or thesis.

### 2. Reading space and wayfinding

- At a 1200 px wide desktop viewport, the main reading column is at least 680 px and usually 700–820 px wide. At 1440 px and wider, prose does not grow past a comfortable measure.
- Persistent navigation never forces body prose below that minimum. If the viewport cannot support the reading column and both navigation areas, navigation becomes on-demand and remains reachable from the page.
- Where desktop side rails appear, **Chapters** and **On this page** can be collapsed independently. Each collapsed rail remains available as a compact labelled control; closing either does not close the other and lets the reading column grow up to 840 px.
- A chapter reader can find the programme path, the current chapter, the page outline and the next/previous chapter without relying on browser history.
- Navigation can be operated with a keyboard, shows a visible focus indicator and exposes its expanded/collapsed state to assistive technology.
- The reading area remains the visual centre. Navigation does not appear as two equally prominent applications framing the article.

### 3. Diagrams and other visuals

- A visual earns its space by explaining a mechanism, comparison, decision or quantity. It does not restate the adjacent paragraph without adding a useful relationship.
- Labels, arrows, values and caveats remain legible at their actual desktop display size. No content is clipped, hidden, overlapped or dependent on hover.
- Captions state the takeaway and distinguish observed results, derived implications and conceptual models.
- Data-bearing charts provide their underlying values in an accessible form. A diagram's visual grammar is consistent across the programme and both site themes.

### 4. Research integrity and usefulness

- Every load-bearing numeric or causal claim can be traced from the rendered page to a source and the source supports the same population, task, conditions and scope claimed in the prose.
- Transfer from general long-context or benchmark results to coding-agent work is called out as inference unless the cited source tested coding agents directly.
- Composite examples are clearly identified as constructed and their assumptions are inspectable. Recommendations derived from them are not presented as measured outcomes.
- The reader can tell what is established, what is an engineering inference, what is practitioner testimony and what remains uncertain.
- Recommendations name a concrete next action and the main cost, failure mode or check that could show the recommendation is wrong.

### 5. Desktop finish

- At 1200 × 900 and 1440 × 900, the reviewed pages have no horizontal page overflow, clipped diagrams, obscured headings, or stuck/overlapping navigation.
- At least the research index, programme map and one chapter are reviewed in-browser after implementation. Representative long-form and diagram-heavy sections receive a second visual pass.
- The research-content review records material evidence or scope defects separately from visual preference. A visual redesign does not count as a content-quality pass.

## Stop rule

The work is ready when every pass condition is evidenced on the reviewed desktop pages, all P1 findings are fixed or explicitly demonstrated to be false, and no remaining P2 issue materially impairs orientation, reading or trust. Any limitation that could not be checked is recorded rather than called passed.
