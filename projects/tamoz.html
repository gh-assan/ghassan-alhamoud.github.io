From monitoring to a response

Tamoz is being built to monitor sensor data, alert teams to changes, bring relevant information together, and recommend a response. Your team reviews the recommendations and decides what to do.

The system can also be tested against recorded or simulated situations. In a pump simulation, verified repairs became a rule after review and testing. The rule could then respond to that pattern without another model call. We would need to test whether this approach is useful for other situations.

One example: a pump station trial

In a simulated eight-pump station, Tamoz examined sensor readings and recommended maintenance for injected faults. In one fault family, verified repairs led to a rule that made five correct, on-time decisions without a model call. The rule did not act on three healthy look-alikes.

The trial covered one simulated station, one seed and three test days. Only one of three fault families produced an approved rule; the trial approver was scripted. These results do not establish savings, reliability, or performance on real equipment. Explore the trial and its limits.

A focused first pilot

We are looking for an industrial team interested in testing Tamoz on a real operational question. We would agree on the scope, terms, and measures together.

  1. Choose a problem. Select a process or asset and agree on the result we want to investigate.
  2. Assess the data. Use existing sensor data where suitable. If it is missing, we can discuss installing sensors with your team and following your site requirements.
  3. Monitor and review. Where useful history exists, replay it. Otherwise, collect a baseline and review live readings, alerts, and recommendations with your team.
  4. Evaluate the results. Compare what we learned with the measures agreed at the start and decide together what to do next.

Depending on the problem, measures could include energy use, material waste, the time spent investigating alerts, or the usefulness of recommendations. The pilot starts read-only. Your team remains in control of decisions. Any later equipment action would require a separate agreement and safeguards.

Where the project stands

Tamoz is an early-stage, open-source project seeking its first operational pilot. The approach has been tested in simulation and on a small hardware bench. We have not yet established savings in a real operation. Explore the evidence, technical details and limitations, or view the public source code.
